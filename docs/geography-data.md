# Household Atlas data and behavior

Games, entered through a feature shortcut or above any household scene, loads a bundled atlas with 242 Natural Earth map entities. These are countries, dependencies, disputed areas and Antarctica, rather than a claim that there are 242 sovereign states. The default quiz uses 195 countries: the [193 UN members](https://research.un.org/en/unmembers/currentmembers) and the [Holy See and Palestine observer states](https://research.un.org/en/unmembers/observers). The explicit catalog is independent of Natural Earth’s entity-type labels. Expanded rounds include all 242 entities. Antarctica appears in expanded World rounds, with no standalone Antarctica category. The quiz uses Natural Earth's de facto boundaries and regional classifications. Russia is classified in Europe; transcontinental states follow the dataset's continent assignment. Microstates and islands have enlarged clickable markers at their geographic locations. Oceania and its subregions rotate the globe toward the Pacific. A plain fallback map uses Pacific-centered geometry.

Country boundaries come from [Natural Earth v5.1.2, 1:50m admin-0 countries](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/v5.1.2/geojson/ne_50m_admin_0_countries.geojson), in the public domain. Shared source segments are deduplicated and drawn as fixed-width vector lines across the terrain mesh. Each edge is split where it crosses a mesh triangle. Borders render as a cartographic overlay with the far hemisphere clipped, avoiding depth conflicts with steep terrain. The terrain outline is 1.25 screen pixels; the paper outline is 1 pixel. Degenerate polar segments are omitted. Country picking uses an analytic ray/sphere intersection, refined with elevation, and tests bounding boxes before the original geographic polygons, so antialiased border pixels cannot select an unrelated country. Small countries and territories have screen-space targets at their geographic locations: a 22-pixel radius for touch and 16 pixels for a mouse, with the nearest visible marker winning. This avoids raycasting the full terrain mesh. Regional filters dim other countries without changing the globe's geography.

The land surface uses actual [NOAA ETOPO1 ice-surface elevation data](https://coastwatch.pfeg.noaa.gov/erddap/info/etopo180/index.html), freely redistributable by NOAA. `scripts/build-elevation.py` samples the 1 arc-minute source at 5-minute intervals: a 4,321 × 2,161 grid, or 9,337,681 elevations (36 times the former half-degree grid). It precomputes an 8,192 × 4,096 color texture and a 4,096 × 2,048 object-space normal texture from the real elevation gradients. These normals provide terrain lighting at roughly 9 km equatorial source spacing without requiring that many mesh vertices. Water normals remain spherical. Ocean color follows bathymetry; the Natural Earth desert polygons guide the sand tint. Other land colors are illustrative latitude/elevation colors, not a vegetation survey.

A separate quarter-degree 1,441 × 721 signed-meter grid, encoded as little-endian int16 with a SHA-256 integrity value, drives the raised mesh. Desktop uses 960 × 480 segments (921,600 triangles). Phones and devices reporting at most 4 GB of memory use 640 × 320 segments, 4K color and 2K normals. Relief and normals use 25× vertical exaggeration. This simplified globe does not preserve every summit and is not intended for elevation measurement.

The globe is rendered locally with Three.js. Smooth slope lighting replaces the former large, flat triangular facets. Country outlines are separate vector geometry; only a small translucent quiz overlay changes with answers, so the terrain textures are not rebuilt or uploaded again. WebP assets (quality-94 color, lossless normals) are versioned by their SHA-256 content hashes and verified by the build and privacy checks. The high-detail color and normal downloads total approximately 6.2 MB; the smaller pair totals 2.0 MB, fetched only when opening Games. The canvas follows the screen pixel ratio up to 2× (1.75× on phones), capped at four million drawing-buffer pixels. Rendering pauses when hidden, offscreen or idle; reduced motion skips camera journeys. A failed or lost graphics context falls back to the plain unlabeled map while retaining the active quiz. No map, elevation, image, or history service is contacted during play.

Close views additionally use native 1 arc-minute ETOPO1 data (21,601 × 10,801 samples), roughly 1.9 km at the equator. `scripts/build-globe-details.py` bakes 30° tiles with a two-sample gutter for filtering across tile edges. The shader blends their color and object-space normals over the global maps. Up to four nearby tiles load as the camera settles below distance 2; unused tiles and image bitmaps are disposed. Resetting, zooming out, hiding Games, or moving elsewhere aborts stale fetches, and late completions cannot restore an obsolete tile. Tile selection wraps around the date line and clamps at the poles. Tiles are supplementary: a failed tile request retains the global terrain and the active quiz. This provides fine surface shading without a globe-wide high-resolution mesh or keeping all high-resolution textures in GPU memory.


Capital, dated population estimates, terrain descriptions and historical excerpts are from the public-domain CIA World Factbook, preserved by [factbook/factbook.json](https://github.com/factbook/factbook.json/tree/144d6977b2b01ac1cbd220de754c0a005616760b). The exact source profile is recorded in each JSON record. The snapshot was retrieved on 2026-11-09; population strings retain the source's own estimate dates. It is an archived reference, not live political or demographic data. Historical episodes are selected by `scripts/geography_history.py`, excluding recent officeholder successions, present-day status reports, unexplained acronyms and excerpts that depend on missing antecedents. It favors older cultural history, settlement, trade, engineering, state formation, colonialism and major conflicts. Selected profiles have concise editorial rewrites with people’s roles explained. The card initially shows one episode; further history is expandable. The original background remains in the dataset for review, but is not displayed. No historical trivia is invented for Siachen Glacier, which lacks a source profile. Terrain cards show the first short physical description, capped at 150 characters. The archived Factbook is not a comprehensive history and reflects its publisher’s perspective.

Most editorial rewrites summarize the same pinned Factbook backgrounds. Additional review sources: [Versailles palace history](https://en.chateauversailles.fr/discover/estate/palace), [UNESCO’s Versailles description](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/83/), [Egypt’s Ministry of Tourism on Memphis](https://egymonuments.gov.eg/world-heritage/memphis-and-its-necropolis/), [UNESCO on Nan Madol](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1503), and [Chief Roi Mata’s Domain](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1280/). The Australian profile corrects the archived background’s erroneous attribution of the 1606 landing to Abel Tasman: the [National Museum of Australia identifies Willem Janszoon](https://www.nma.gov.au/defining-moments/resources/janszoon-maps-northern-australian-coast). UNESCO-derived passages are paraphrases; their descriptions are licensed CC BY-SA IGO 3.0. Source links remain in this documentation rather than the game cards.

Åland, Northern Cyprus and Somaliland have separate editorial context rather than inheriting Finland's, Cyprus's or Somalia's population and capital. These retain Natural Earth's dated population estimates. Siachen Glacier has geographic context rather than invented historical facts. Review references for the editorial context: [Åland's autonomy](https://um.fi/the-special-status-of-the-aland-islands), [UNFICYP background](https://unficyp.unmissions.org/en/about-unficyp), and [UK Parliament’s Somaliland briefing](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-9733/CBP-9733.pdf). Historical source links are deliberately absent from the application at the user's request.

Run `uv run scripts/build-elevation.py` for the global terrain, followed by `uv run scripts/build-globe-details.py` for close-up tiles; use `python3 scripts/build-geography.py` to reproduce `src/geography-data.json`. Download caches remain in ignored `.local/geography`; normal builds never fetch external data. This is a separate lazy-loaded game bundle and does not add atlas data to other tabs or the house renderer.

Answers accept curated aliases and normalize case, accents and punctuation. Autocomplete ranks exact, prefix, word and fuzzy matches within the selected region and country set. Choosing a suggestion immediately submits it, including with Arrow keys and Enter. Free text can still be submitted with Enter or the submit button. Pointer gestures must end on the same option without movement or scrolling before a suggestion is chosen. The answer strip stays above the map and follows `visualViewport` height and offset when an on-screen keyboard opens. Touch selection leaves keyboard opening to the input tap. Keyboard users rotate with arrows, select the central reticle with Enter, zoom with +/−, and reset with Home. The fallback map supports pan, pinch, screen-size island markers, and Tab/Enter selection.

**Random country** selects and zooms to an unanswered place. **Reset view** restores the chosen region without changing answers, lives or the clock. Practice rounds can be untimed or 1/3/5/10/15 minutes, with unlimited/3/5/10 lives. The first selection starts the clock; a wrong submitted answer costs one life. Time continues through navigation and reloads. Changing region, mode or country set requires starting a new round in Game settings. Player changes restore that player’s saved progress. Completed answers are reviewed from the settings menu. Existing saves without country-set metadata are migrated as Expanded; an old Antarctica-only save becomes expanded World and retains its Antarctica answer.

**Study map**, in Settings, opens a separate labeled flat map without starting or changing a round. Region and country-set filters are independent of quiz settings. Choose a country to zoom in, or pan and zoom directly; labels avoid collisions and more appear as space permits. Learning countdowns pause while studying. During an active competitive round the study map is unavailable; finish the round or switch to Practice first. It uses the existing vector boundaries without terrain downloads or a second WebGL renderer.

**Learn** uses a Fisher–Yates shuffle of the chosen pool, persisted per person. It prompts with a country name and waits for a map tap. After 12 seconds of visible play without a correct tap, or when **Show me** is chosen, it fills the country, focuses the camera and places a name label at its center. Shown answers stay separate from correct ones. The result waits for **Next**; countries do not repeat within a round. The countdown pauses in menus, on the continue/score screens, when the game is hidden and when the document is hidden. Reloads preserve the current prompt and approximate remaining time (saved each second).

**Competitive · name countries** retains the tap-and-name quiz. **Competitive · find countries** prompts with shuffled country names and accepts map taps. Every guess advances immediately, including a wrong country or empty-map tap; drags and zoom gestures are not guesses. Countries are attempted once per round. A missed target costs a life and is not counted as correct. The queue and missed targets survive reloads. Both competitive modes fix every round at five minutes and three lives. Assistance is unavailable. A correct answer is worth 100 points and a mistake costs 25, with a zero minimum. Completed rounds (time, lives or the entire country set attempted) are saved once using the round’s unique ID. The leaderboard at `#games/scores` is a separate screen, with a best result per player, challenge, region and country set. It orders by points, then correct answers, then elapsed milliseconds, then finish date for deterministic ties. Time-based endings use the actual deadline even if the tab was closed. Practice and learning never submit scores.

Migration `029_geography_scores.sql` stores a small country catalog and append-only results in `ledger_private`; direct public table access is revoked and RLS is enabled. Narrow RPCs validate the player, category, pool size, numeric bounds and a completed-round condition, calculate points in SQL, and handle duplicate retries atomically. Results follow the existing household’s selected-name trust model, not authenticated esports anti-cheat. The frontend keeps an outbox and retries on startup, reconnection or leaderboard refresh. Failed loads explicitly show device-only data and pending entries. Guest results are local only. No game RPC changes household accounting or chore points. Migration `030_geography_find_scores.sql` adds the challenge category, retains existing scores as naming results, and accepts a finished find round with fewer than three mistakes when every target was attempted. Legacy score outbox retries remain valid. The release schema check requires migration 030.

Games retains `#games` for the single-button Continue screen and `#games/geography` for play. It remains outside the six household destinations and horizontal rotation order. Upward wheel/touch gestures, Page Up and the Games button enter from all six house scenes. A cancellable cloud ascent uses the house renderer; reduced motion skips it. Every household feature has a direct Games shortcut. A labeled return link on the hub, game and score screen restores the last household route and its scene/feature view in this browser tab; fresh direct entries return to To-dos.

**Terrain / Paper** is in Game settings and is saved on this device. Paper uses a 256 × 128 smooth cream sphere without terrain image downloads. Changing style preserves the camera, answers, lives and clock; Paper suspends and releases detail tiles. Terrain retains the existing bounded ETOPO1 textures and detail tiles. Forest/glacier land-cover rendering is deferred: current non-desert land colors are illustrative, and no extra geometry or imagery was added in this update.

Untimed practice offers **Hint** and **Reveal**. Hints begin with the capital, followed by a masked name with word initials. They preserve input and do not cost a life. A triple-click/tap can also reveal a country. Revealed answers turn amber and are excluded from the correct count and Random country pool. A round resolved partly through reveals ends as complete rather than an all-correct win. Timed practice and competitive rounds disable assistance.
