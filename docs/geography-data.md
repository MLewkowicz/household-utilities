# Household Atlas data and behavior

Games, entered above Home, loads a bundled atlas with 242 Natural Earth map entities. These are countries, dependencies, disputed areas and Antarctica, rather than a claim that there are 242 sovereign states. The quiz uses Natural Earth's de facto boundaries and regional classifications. Russia is classified in Europe; transcontinental states follow the dataset's continent assignment. Microstates and islands have enlarged clickable markers at their geographic locations. Oceania and its subregions rotate the globe toward the Pacific. A plain fallback map uses Pacific-centered geometry.

Country boundaries come from [Natural Earth v5.1.2, 1:50m admin-0 countries](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector/blob/v5.1.2/geojson/ne_50m_admin_0_countries.geojson), in the public domain. Shared source segments are deduplicated and drawn as fixed-width vector lines across the terrain mesh. Each edge is split where it crosses a mesh triangle. Borders render as a cartographic overlay with the far hemisphere clipped, avoiding depth conflicts with steep terrain. The terrain outline is 1.25 screen pixels; the paper outline is 1 pixel. Degenerate polar segments are omitted. Country picking tests the original geographic polygons at the ray’s intersection with Earth, so antialiased border pixels cannot select an unrelated country. Small territories have enlarged circular targets at their geographic locations. Regional filters dim other countries without changing the globe's geography.

The land surface uses actual [NOAA ETOPO1 ice-surface elevation data](https://coastwatch.pfeg.noaa.gov/erddap/info/etopo180/index.html), freely redistributable by NOAA. `scripts/build-elevation.py` samples the 1 arc-minute source at 5-minute intervals: a 4,321 × 2,161 grid, or 9,337,681 elevations (36 times the former half-degree grid). It precomputes an 8,192 × 4,096 color texture and a 4,096 × 2,048 object-space normal texture from the real elevation gradients. These normals provide terrain lighting at roughly 9 km equatorial source spacing without requiring that many mesh vertices. Water normals remain spherical. Ocean color follows bathymetry; the Natural Earth desert polygons guide the sand tint. Other land colors are illustrative latitude/elevation colors, not a vegetation survey.

A separate quarter-degree 1,441 × 721 signed-meter grid, encoded as little-endian int16 with a SHA-256 integrity value, drives the raised mesh. Desktop uses 960 × 480 segments (921,600 triangles). Phones and devices reporting at most 4 GB of memory use 640 × 320 segments, 4K color and 2K normals. Relief and normals use 25× vertical exaggeration. This simplified globe does not preserve every summit and is not intended for elevation measurement.

The globe is rendered locally with Three.js. Smooth slope lighting replaces the former large, flat triangular facets. Country outlines are separate vector geometry; only a small translucent quiz overlay changes with answers, so the terrain textures are not rebuilt or uploaded again. WebP assets (quality-94 color, lossless normals) are versioned by their SHA-256 content hashes and verified by the build and privacy checks. The high-detail color and normal downloads total approximately 6.2 MB; the smaller pair totals 2.0 MB, fetched only when opening Games. The canvas follows the screen pixel ratio up to 2.5×, capped at six million drawing-buffer pixels. Rendering pauses when hidden, offscreen or idle; reduced motion skips camera journeys. A failed or lost graphics context falls back to the plain unlabeled map while retaining the active quiz. No map, elevation, image, or history service is contacted during play.

Close views additionally use native 1 arc-minute ETOPO1 data (21,601 × 10,801 samples), roughly 1.9 km at the equator. `scripts/build-globe-details.py` bakes 30° tiles with a two-sample gutter for filtering across tile edges. The shader blends their color and object-space normals over the global maps. Up to four nearby tiles load as the camera settles below distance 2; unused tiles and image bitmaps are disposed. Resetting, zooming out, hiding Games, or moving elsewhere aborts stale fetches, and late completions cannot restore an obsolete tile. Tile selection wraps around the date line and clamps at the poles. Tiles are supplementary: a failed tile request retains the global terrain and the active quiz. This provides fine surface shading without a globe-wide high-resolution mesh or keeping all high-resolution textures in GPU memory.


Capital, dated population estimates, terrain descriptions and historical excerpts are from the public-domain CIA World Factbook, preserved by [factbook/factbook.json](https://github.com/factbook/factbook.json/tree/144d6977b2b01ac1cbd220de754c0a005616760b). The exact source profile is recorded in each JSON record. The snapshot was retrieved on 2026-11-09; population strings retain the source's own estimate dates. It is an archived reference, not live political or demographic data. Four historical sentences are selected from each profile's background, favoring dated state formation, empires, independence, colonialism, major wars and atrocities, in their original order. Profiles with less material have fewer excerpts. The complete background is retained in the dataset for review. These are excerpts, not a comprehensive history; the Factbook reflects its publisher's perspective.

Åland, Northern Cyprus and Somaliland have separate editorial context rather than inheriting Finland's, Cyprus's or Somalia's population and capital. These retain Natural Earth's dated population estimates. Siachen Glacier has geographic context rather than invented historical facts. Review references for the editorial context: [Åland's autonomy](https://um.fi/the-special-status-of-the-aland-islands), [UNFICYP background](https://unficyp.unmissions.org/en/about-unficyp), and [UK Parliament’s Somaliland briefing](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-9733/CBP-9733.pdf). Historical source links are deliberately absent from the application at the user's request.

Run `uv run scripts/build-elevation.py` for the global terrain, followed by `uv run scripts/build-globe-details.py` for close-up tiles; use `python3 scripts/build-geography.py` to reproduce `src/geography-data.json`. Download caches remain in ignored `.local/geography`; normal builds never fetch external data. This is a separate lazy-loaded game bundle and does not add atlas data to other tabs or the house renderer.

Answers accept curated aliases and normalize case, accents and punctuation. Autocomplete ranks exact, prefix, word and fuzzy matches within the selected region. A suggestion fills the answer; **Check answer** submits it. This keeps a suggestion tap from accidentally spending a life. The globe stays unlabeled until an answer reveals its country card. Keyboard users rotate with arrows, select the country under the central reticle with Enter, zoom with +/−, and reset with Home. The fallback map supports Tab and Enter for country selection.

**Random country** selects and zooms to an unanswered place. **Reset view** restores the selected region’s preset orientation and camera distance while preserving the answer, score, lives and clock. Rounds can be untimed or 1/3/5/10/15 minutes, with unlimited/3/5/10 lives. The first selection starts the clock; a wrong submitted answer costs one life. Time continues in the hub, during navigation, and through reloads. The hub’s Reset control returns to its world preset. A completed round locks new answers but retains its learned country cards for review. Changing regions after play has begun opens round settings, which explicitly says starting again clears this round's progress. Device-local storage is separate for each household member and Guest; it does not synchronize between devices.

Games keeps a direct `#games` link, with `#games/geography` for the quiz. Upward wheel/touch gestures and PageUp work only at the top of Home. The visible sky-entry button offers the same route without a gesture. The journey can be cancelled while loading, and stale completions cannot navigate after cancellation. The Home control returns Home; downward scrolling from Home still enters To-dos. Games is not part of the six house destinations or the horizontal rotation order.

The **Terrain / Paper** control is available in the hub and quiz. Paper uses a lighter 256 × 128 smooth cream sphere and ink outlines, with no terrain color map, elevation or relief lighting. The choice is saved on this device. Starting in Paper avoids all terrain image downloads; switching to Paper cancels detail requests and releases their textures. Style changes preserve the camera, selection, score, lives and clock. The fallback map supports the same choice.

Untimed rounds offer progressive **Hint** and **Reveal** controls. Hints show the country's subregion (when it differs from the chosen category), capital, and a masked name with its word initials. They preserve the current input and do not cost a life. A triple-click/tap on the same country also reveals it; the Reveal button offers a keyboard and touch alternative. Revealed countries turn amber, receive a separate count and review list, and are excluded from Random country and the remaining count. They never increase the correct-answer count. A round with all countries completed through answers or reveals ends as complete, separately from an all-correct win. Timed rounds disable both assistance methods. Hints and reveals persist per person, with old saved rounds upgraded without losing correct answers.
